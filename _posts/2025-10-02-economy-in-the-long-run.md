## TL;DR

- In the long run, output depends on the stock of capital, labour, and how productively they're combined (TFP).
- Short-run fluctuations wash out; what's left is the production function.
- Policy levers in the long run look very different from the short run — it's savings, human capital, and institutions, not rate cuts.

## What Session 1 covered

This is the opener of the series. Prof. Tantri sets up the long-run framework that the rest of the course argues against, builds on, or qualifies. Topics touched in session:

- The aggregate production function — `Y = A · F(K, L)`
- What `A` actually is (TFP) and why it's the residual that explains most of why rich countries are rich.
- Capital accumulation and the role of savings.
- Why the long run is "boring" — and why that's the point.
