The Wage Watch

The missing variable.

₹41 lakh crore of new money either becomes GDP or becomes prices, and the tell is not "are wages rising." It is the reservation wage — the raise below which your people start sending out CVs, adjusted for productivity. Nobody measures it. The RBI surveys households who don't follow the question; IIM-Ahmedabad asks informed people for a forecast, and informed people just repeat the current print. You are the only ones who actually know. Two minutes. Anonymous — no name, no email, no account id stored, ever.

The kind no terminal sells — it exists only because people on the ground write it down. The aggregate goes to class once the sample is worth discussing, and feeds the Money Printer. Come back next month; this is a series, not a poll.

Why this exists. Session 19's fork: if the past year's money growth meets idle capacity it becomes output; if it meets a rising wage floor it becomes inflation, durably, because expectations move and then prices follow. The professor spent the last fifteen minutes of that class designing this measurement — and rejecting the versions that already exist. "I want to understand whether your reservation wage has gone up without any increase in productivity… If you can identify it before it starts, you will be doing great service to the policymakers, your companies, and your clients." And on what it could become: "If you can create a group and launch something, this will be tracked by markets. Imagine if it starts influencing bond prices tomorrow." The full session notes carry the argument; this page is the instrument.

One known limitation, raised in class. Inflation is not one number — above ₹10 lakh a 5–7% hike is judged against school fees rising 12%; below it, the same arithmetic bites harder. So the vantage and sector questions above are not decoration: they let the aggregate be cut by band rather than flattened into a single misleading average.
