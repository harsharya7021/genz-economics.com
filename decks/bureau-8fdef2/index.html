02The gap

Foreigners own India's macro story. Indians own the exam prep.

IMPORTED

Explained to us, by them

Search "India economy explained" and the top results are Economics Explained and Money & Macro — foreign channels explaining India to Indians, and doing it well.

FACE-LED

The domestic giant

Think School, 5.3M — business and geopolitics case studies, now drifting into 47–82 minute masterclasses. Excellent. Also unparallelisable and unsellable, because it is a person.

EXAM PREP

Syllabus, not mechanism

StudyIQ, PW OnlyIAS and the UPSC machine own Indian econ search. They teach what will be asked. Nobody teaches how it works.

THE SLOT

Faceless, weekly, documentary-grade

The closest thing is Full Disclosure (466K) — 3–4 week cadence, no recurring series. Finfluencers now carry SEBI risk; sponsors want brand-safe finance inventory and can't find it. Formats lag the US by 3–5 years. That window is the trade.

Demand isn't the question. "Entire Indian Economics in 18 Mins" has 2.2M views. A channel called Explained In Minutes has 122K subscribers and two public videos. The appetite is proven; the supply isn't there.

03What we make

One faceless channel. Six kinds of video.

Explainers

Historical documentaries

Origin stories

Stories & facts

The good stories economics is full of — told properly, and sourced.

"$130bn a year comes home" · "The princely state that printed its own rupee"

05 · CASE STUDIES

Case-study breakdowns

One company, one deal, one circular — how it actually works, or why it failed.

"₹10 trillion hidden in an RBI circular" · "Why Jet Airways died and IndiGo didn't"

06 · ECONOMY BREAKDOWNS

Economy breakdowns

A country, a state or a sector in one sitting — what it makes, and where the money goes.

"How Vietnam became the factory" · "How Japan ran a $2 trillion carry trade"

Add-ons, not the core — settled on the call

Every two weeks: what just happened, and the mechanism behind it. Recorded at ISB's studio. "Fortnightly is perfectly fine" — your words. The library works without it.

ADD-ON B · STALWART SPECIALS

Occasional guests — an event, not a format

The pillars are a topic map, not six shows — the channel page reads as one library. Later optionality, parked: an IAS-officer series, a decision-maker brief. Neither is in the plan or the budget.

09The audience & the money

The RPM case is a geography case.

Indian finance RPM runs roughly $0.50–$1.60 — five to fifteen per cent of the US. We'd rather show you the low number than quote you an American one you'd see straight through.

The mechanism that changes it: English-language economics pulls tier-1 traffic. Indian creators with 60%+ US/UK/AU audiences report $4–10 RPM. We write an Indian story in English for a global audience — and India's macro is currently being explained to the world by people who don't live here.

10The production line

Forty-five to ninety hours a video. That is the whole business.

StageWhat happensHours
Research & scriptTopic from the bank, primary sources, 2,000–2,700 words. The moat.12–25
Review & fact-checkRetention structure, hook, and the professor's accuracy pass3–5
Voice-overHuman narration, on retainer3–5
Motion & editCharts, data-viz, archival, sound — the bottleneck and the quality signal25–50
Thumbnail & packaging3–5 variants, title testing, publish3–6
Where AI helps, and where it does notIt compresses 20–35% of hours, almost all in pre-production — source triage, transcript rough-cuts, thumbnail variants, captioning. It does not do data visualisation, and generative video is unusable as primary footage. More importantly: YouTube's July 2025 policy demonetises templated, mass-produced output. Original script plus real curation isn't just our thesis — it's the compliance line. Human voice-over costs 2–4% of budget and removes 100% of the "is this AI slop?" risk.

19Risks

What could go wrong, and what's already built against it.

Platform

The AI-slop crackdown

YouTube now demonetises templated, mass-produced output. Our defence is the product itself: original scripts from primary research, human narration, bespoke charts. The compliance line and the thesis are the same line.

Niche size

Econ is smaller than comedy

True, and Indian finance RPM is low. The answer is geography and sponsors, not scale — and a library that keeps earning. We'd rather own a small category than rent a big one.

Key person

The professor's time

Mitigated by design — he reviews, he doesn't operate. The topic bank exists independently of him. If he steps back the library and the formats survive; only the podcast pauses.

Execution

Consistency is the killer

The barrier he named. Four videos banked before launch, a three-video buffer at all times, formats judged in 8-episode windows. The operating rules are written down because the last attempt died of inconsistency.
